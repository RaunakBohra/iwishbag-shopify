Based on recent cross‑border e‑commerce research, the following areas should be considered:
	•	Digital wallet & UPI‑style payments:  Digital wallets already handle about 60 % of global digital transactions, and they’re projected to account for over 51 % of cross‑border e‑commerce payments by 2025 ￼.  In India, UPI accounts for roughly 55 % of online transactions ￼, and gateways like Razorpay and PayU now offer global payment services.  Adding support for UPI and popular Indian wallets (PhonePe, Google Pay, Paytm) alongside international methods (Stripe, PayPal, Apple/Google Pay) will be critical.
	•	Logistics for India and beyond:  The platform currently integrates Nepalese couriers.  Indian providers such as Delhivery, Blue Dart and Shiprocket have built international shipping solutions with competitive pricing and modern API support ￼.  As you expand, you’ll need connectors for these carriers as well as global networks like DHL, FedEx or ShipBob to handle cross‑border fulfilment ￼.  Features like automated customs paperwork, AI‑optimized routing and real‑time tracking will enhance the user experience ￼.
	•	Tax compliance and invoicing:  Nepal VAT invoices are covered, but India imposes GST (with e‑invoice and TCS obligations) and other countries have their own VAT/GST schemes.  The system should support multi‑country tax rules, multiple tax IDs per merchant and automatic calculation of duties for international shipments.  Integration with India’s GST e‑invoice APIs will likely be required for domestic sellers.
	•	Multi‑currency support:  To serve customers and merchants across borders, your database and pricing logic should support multiple currencies (INR, USD, EUR, NPR, etc.) and real‑time FX conversions.  Displaying prices in a buyer’s local currency reduces cart abandonment.
	•	Language and localization:  The platform is bilingual (English/Nepali) today.  Expansion to India will require Hindi and other major languages, and further expansion should plan for local languages in each target market.  This includes not only text translation but also right‑to‑left scripts where necessary and locale‑specific formatting.
	•	Compliance with local regulations:  India’s Data Protection Act (DPDP 2023) and other countries’ privacy laws (GDPR, CCPA) impose strict data‑handling rules.  You’ll need configurable retention policies, consent management and cross‑border data‑transfer safeguards.  Also consider integrating with the Indian government’s Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC), which standardizes cataloguing, order management and settlement.
	•	Mobile‑first and PWA/native apps:  Mobile purchases will represent about 59 % of e‑commerce sales by 2025 ￼, and 73 % of shoppers prefer native apps over mobile web ￼.  Investing in a Progressive Web App or dedicated Android/iOS apps (with push‑notification support and offline capabilities) will help reach mobile‑first consumers.
	•	AI‑powered personalisation and dynamic pricing:  AI can recommend products, forecast inventory, adjust pricing in real‑time and provide multilingual chat support ￼.  Major retailers expect AI‑driven personalisation to contribute significantly to revenue ￼.  Incorporating AI tools (either through third‑party services or your own models) will make your platform competitive in India and internationally.
	•	Buy‑Now‑Pay‑Later (BNPL) and alternative financing:  BNPL services (Simpl, ZestMoney in India, Afterpay, Klarna globally) are growing quickly and appeal to younger buyers.  Integrating BNPL providers alongside credit cards and digital wallets will widen your customer base.
	•	Returns management and cross‑border compliance:  International expansion introduces complex return logistics, customs duties and fraud risk.  Features for seamless returns, fraud detection, address validation and cross‑border customer support will be important.
	•	Scalability and new country onboarding:  Beyond India, each country will bring new payment gateways, languages, logistics partners and legal requirements.  Designing your integration layer (for payments, shipping, tax, languages) to be plug‑in based will make adding a new country faster.

Overall, your current roadmap is comprehensive for the Nepalese market and sets a strong foundation.  The additions above—particularly UPI/digital wallets, India‑focused logistics, multi‑currency/tax support, stronger localization, mobile apps, AI personalisation and regulatory compliance—will help ensure a smooth expansion into India and other markets.
